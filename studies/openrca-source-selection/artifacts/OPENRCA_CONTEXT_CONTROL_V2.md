# OpenRCA ContextControl-v2

## Research question

Can an action-conditioned controller selectively acquire OpenRCA telemetry sources and improve the reliability–cost frontier relative to no-context, fixed retrieval, and always-fetch policies?

Track: `controller`, `intervention`, and `cost-efficiency`.

## Method

ContextControl-v2 consumes only leakage-safe, out-of-fold modality predictions from the canonical action model. Each incident is a fixed hidden state. The policy begins without incident telemetry and can acquire metric, trace, application, middleware, or correlated duplicate-metric sources before returning a top-3 component shortlist or abstaining.

The paired intervention suite includes canonical, trace-unavailable, high-trace-cost, stale-metric, wrong-entity-metric, and temporal-gap conditions. The sufficiency gate and source-outcome ranker are separate logistic models. Every test date is held out; two preceding dates calibrate stopping from trajectories generated by the deployment acquisition policy.

## Canonical results

| Policy | Acceptable rate | Coverage | Accuracy when attempted | Mean calls | Mean cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No context | 54.9% | 100% | 54.9% | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| Always fetch all | 76.5% | 100% | 76.5% | 5.00 | 6.80 |
| Fixed metric + trace | **84.3%** | 100% | **84.3%** | 2.00 | 3.00 |
| Learned source ranker, two calls | **84.3%** | 100% | **84.3%** | 2.00 | **2.56** |
| Deployment-calibrated gate + ranker | 64.7% | 80.4% | 80.5% | 1.84 | 2.74 |
| Oracle cheapest sufficient subset | 88.2% | 88.2% | 100% | 0.94 | 1.29 |

Selective metric/trace acquisition improves canonical reliability by 7.8 percentage points over always-fetch while reducing acquisition cost by 55.9–62.3%. In paired comparison it wins four incidents, loses zero, and ties 47; the bootstrap interval for the accuracy gain is 2.0–15.7 percentage points.

## Paired intervention aggregate

Across 306 episodes (51 incidents × six conditions):

| Policy | Acceptable rate | Unsafe-action rate | Coverage | Accuracy when attempted | Mean cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No context | 54.9% | 45.1% | 100% | 54.9% | 0.00 |
| Always fetch | 73.9% | 26.1% | 100% | 73.9% | 7.47 |
| Source ranker, one call | 75.5% | 24.5% | 100% | 75.5% | 1.81 |
| Source ranker, two calls | **79.1%** | **20.9%** | 100% | **79.1%** | 3.18 |
| Cost-aware ranker (λ=0.10) | 75.8% | 24.2% | 100% | 75.8% | **2.31** |
| Guarded VOI controller | 73.2% | 26.8% | 100% | 73.2% | **1.86** |
| Deployment-calibrated gate + ranker | 56.2% | **10.5%** | 66.7% | **84.3%** | 3.72 |
| Oracle | 84.3% | 0% | 84.3% | 100% | 1.67 |

The two-source ranker is the best full-coverage learned policy. Additional calls degrade reliability: ranker k3, k4, and k5 achieve 76.8%, 75.8%, and 73.9%. This is direct evidence that more context is not monotonically better.

## Risk–coverage frontier

Increasing the fixed sufficiency threshold produces the expected risk–coverage trade-off:

| Threshold | Coverage | Attempted accuracy | Unsafe-action rate | Mean cost |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.30 | 95.1% | 71.5% | 27.1% | 1.49 |
| 0.40 | 86.6% | 82.3% | 15.4% | 2.62 |
| 0.50 | 75.5% | 84.0% | 12.1% | 3.29 |
| 0.60 | 61.1% | 87.7% | 7.5% | 3.96 |
| 0.70 | 53.3% | 93.3% | 3.6% | 4.32 |
| 0.80 | 37.9% | 97.4% | 1.0% | 5.11 |
| 0.90 | 29.7% | 98.9% | 0.3% | 5.47 |

The sufficiency model has AUROC 0.748, AUPRC 0.898, Brier score 0.201, and ECE 0.143. Discrimination is useful, but calibration remains too weak for a high-coverage safety gate.

## Provenance, freshness, entity, and routing audits

Adding a correlated duplicate of metric evidence changes predicted sufficiency by −0.013 on average and changes action accuracy by 0.0 points. Adding independent trace evidence raises predicted sufficiency by 0.241 and action accuracy by 19.6 points. The estimator therefore does not double-count the explicit correlated duplicate in this test.

The unconstrained ranker learns a mostly fixed modality order and selects metric evidence in 100% of stale and wrong-entity conditions. This fails the dynamic-routing guardrail even though aggregate accuracy remains high.

The guarded VOI controller enforces entity and freshness requirements before computing source value:

- stale metric selected: 0%;
- wrong-entity metric selected: 0%;
- temporal-gap telemetry selected: 0%;
- canonical accuracy: 80.4% at mean cost 1.93;
- aggregate accuracy: 73.2% at mean cost 1.86.

The λ=0.10 cost-aware ranker avoids the expensive trace condition entirely and still exceeds always-fetch aggregate accuracy (75.8% versus 73.9%) at 69.1% lower cost. The guarded VOI policy still selects the expensive trace in 62.7% of those episodes, so cost calibration is not fully solved.

## Conclusion

This experiment provides positive evidence for the source-selection portion of the north-star claim on OpenRCA component triage: a learned two-source policy is more reliable and materially cheaper than always-fetch, and unnecessary sources measurably reduce action quality.

It does not yet validate the full claim. The stopping gate obtains lower unsafe-action rates through abstention but misses 20.3% of episodes where its eventual action would have been correct. The learned router needs explicit hard guards to respect freshness and entity scope, and cost calibration is incomplete. Human probing, authorization, policy sources, reason/time RCA, and natural provenance conflicts remain outside OpenRCA’s coverage.

The scope-valid claim is:

> For OpenRCA component triage, action-conditioned selection of metric and trace context improves both reliability and acquisition cost relative to broad retrieval; learned sufficiency gating yields a controllable risk–coverage frontier but is not yet sufficiently calibrated for high-coverage deployment.
